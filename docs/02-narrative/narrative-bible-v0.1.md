# Narrative Bible v0.1

## 1. Core theme

Cửu Vực is not fundamentally a story about a hero saving the world.

It is about a human being who gradually becomes powerful enough to change the fate of civilization and then has to confront a more difficult question:

> **Chỉ vì ta có thể quyết định, liệu ta có quyền quyết định thay tất cả mọi người?**

The setting rejects a simple absolute boundary between good and evil. People act through circumstance, loss, responsibility, belief, fear, love, interest and limited knowledge.

A decision can save one person and condemn thousands. A dictator can create real peace. Refusing to act can still cause harm.

The game presents choices and consequences, not moral grades.

---

## 2. The protagonist

The protagonist comes from **Làng An Mộc, Thanh Mộc Vực**.

They are not a chosen one and are not born with a divine mission.

Their father is a **Người Dẫn Mạch**, responsible for inspecting and maintaining infrastructure connected to the Linh Mạch.

At the beginning, the protagonist believes in a relatively simple moral framework:

- good people protect others;
- people who knowingly hurt innocents are wrong.

The intended character arc is:

**certainty → doubt → conviction → collapse → self-awareness**

The player does not simply choose whether the protagonist becomes “good” or “evil”. The player shapes what kind of person they become when forced to accept responsibility for decisions that have no clean answer.

---

## 3. The protagonist's hidden origin

As a child, the protagonist suffered from Linh Suy and was expected to die.

Their father used a forbidden method to implant a fragment of Linh Mạch into the child.

The procedure worked.

But obtaining and using that fragment caused an incident in which other people died or suffered lasting consequences.

The father chose his own child's life over the lives of strangers.

This same child later grows up judging others for making similarly compromised decisions.

The protagonist's ability to absorb **Mạch Ấn** is not destiny. It is a consequence of that forbidden intervention.

Their very survival is therefore already rooted in moral ambiguity.

---

## 4. Người Đồng Hành

The protagonist grows up with a person deeply important to them, provisionally called **Người Đồng Hành**.

They may ultimately be written as a sibling-like figure or childhood companion. Their exact identity will be locked in the Character Bible.

At first they fight together.

Over time their worldview diverges.

The protagonist initially believes:

> Innocent people must not be sacrificed for a larger goal.

Người Đồng Hành increasingly believes:

> Refusing to sacrifice some people can become a decision to let everyone die.

Neither position is treated as objectively correct.

Người Đồng Hành eventually aligns with **Quy Nguyên Hội**, becoming simultaneously ally, rival, emotional anchor and philosophical mirror.

---

## 5. The father

The protagonist's first objective is simple:

**Find their missing father.**

For a large part of the early game, the player assumes he was abducted, killed or coerced.

The truth is that he left voluntarily.

He is an important member of Quy Nguyên Hội.

He believes Linh Suy cannot be solved by preserving the current world structure.

His worldview is consistent with the choice he made years ago when he saved his child at the expense of strangers:

> Some decisions must be made even if the person making them has to live with the guilt forever.

He does not need to believe he is morally pure. He believes refusing responsibility is worse than carrying guilt.

# 6. Main story spine

## PROLOGUE — AN MỘC

The protagonist begins in ordinary village life.

Tutorial mechanics are embedded into routine activities: movement, interaction, basic combat, family and local NPC relationships.

The father leaves to investigate a Linh Mạch station and does not return.

That night the forest changes.

Wild animals flee. The ecosystem becomes violent. The journey begins.

---

## CHAPTER I — THANH MỘC

The protagonist enters the forest looking for the father.

Main boss: **Mộc Giác**, an ancient guardian beast suffering because the local Linh Mạch has been damaged.

After defeating it, the protagonist discovers the first major truth:

**The Linh Mạch was not merely failing. It was deliberately cut.**

The protagonist absorbs the first Mạch Ấn.

---

## CHAPTER II — TRUNG VỰC

The protagonist travels to **Thiên Kinh** believing powerful institutions will fix the problem once shown evidence.

Instead they encounter bureaucracy, competing factions, political interests, schools and attempts to suppress the truth.

Quy Nguyên Hội enters the story.

A dangerous possibility is introduced:

**Cutting Linh Mạch may not have caused Linh Suy.**

---

## CHAPTER III — XÍCH SA

Xích Sa depends on Mạch wells for water.

As the region fails, its ruler diverts Linh resources away from small settlements in order to keep the central city alive.

Thousands survive because of this decision.

Others are knowingly abandoned.

This is the first major moral choice with no solution that saves everyone.

**Xích Vương** may become a boss or remain an ally depending on the player's actions.

---

## CHAPTER IV — LÔI TRẠCH

A well-intended restoration attempt results in mass casualties.

The truth becomes harder to ignore: Quy Nguyên Hội did not create Linh Suy. They are accelerating processes already underway.

Người Đồng Hành starts openly sympathizing with their argument.

The relationship fractures.

---

## CHAPTER V — TRUNG VỰC: BIẾN LOẠN

Linh Suy becomes public and Thiên Kinh enters political chaos.

The protagonist finds the father and learns he joined Quy Nguyên Hội voluntarily.

Neither can persuade the other.

The protagonist then encounters **Thủ Mạch Nhân**, a surviving figure from the era of Đại Phân Ly.

### Scripted loss

The boss fight is real and must be mechanically credible.

Phase 1 can be beaten.

After the apparent victory, Thủ Mạch Nhân stops holding back. The normal boss health structure disappears and the tools the player has relied upon are systematically countered.

The player is defeated without a Game Over state and the story continues.

The encounter communicates two ideas:

1. no level of human power is necessarily absolute;
2. combat strength does not grant moral authority over the world's future.

---

## CHAPTER VI — HÀN SƠN

The protagonist searches frozen battlefields for unaltered records of the past.

Main boss: **Bạch Tướng**.

Reveal: Đại Phân Ly was deliberate.

However, its creators were not purely heroic. After saving the world, their descendants used control over the divided Mạch to create political, religious and martial power structures that lasted for centuries.

The same action both saved humanity and enabled systemic injustice.

---

## CHAPTER VII — HUYỀN HẢI

Ancient ruins below the sea preserve records untouched by later regimes.

Major reveal:

**The Cửu Mạch genuinely are dying.**

If nothing is done, civilization will eventually collapse.

The story can no longer be framed as simply stopping villains from breaking a necessary seal.

---

## CHAPTER VIII — VÂN HẢI

Người Đồng Hành has become a major figure aligned with Quy Nguyên Hội.

The reunion is not driven by hatred. Both characters still care about one another but no longer believe in the same future.

An ideological duel occurs. Depending on previous state, the player may cooperate, refuse, fight, spare or permanently sever the relationship.

This choice has major endgame consequences.

---

## CHAPTER IX — U MINH

U Minh forces the protagonist to confront memory, death and personal responsibility.

The player encounters memories or spiritual remnants of people who died because of earlier decisions, as well as those who survived because of them.

The protagonist discovers the truth of their childhood illness and the father's forbidden intervention.

Their worldview collapses.

They can no longer honestly claim they would never make the compromises they condemned in others because their own life is the product of one.

---

## CHAPTER X — THIÊN KHUYẾT

The protagonist discovers the civilization that existed before Đại Phân Ly.

Ancient mechanisms reveal that **Nguyên Linh is not inherently evil**.

It reflects and magnifies will.

The original purpose of dividing the world was not merely to imprison a monster. It was also to prevent any single will from controlling the source of the Cửu Mạch.

The danger is therefore inseparable from a political and philosophical question:

**Who gets to decide what the world needs?**

---

## CHAPTER XI — CỬU MẠCH

Linh Suy reaches a point where collapse can no longer be hidden.

All nine realms enter crisis.

Earlier choices return as world state:

- NPCs previously saved may appear again;
- people spared may help or betray the protagonist;
- communities sacrificed earlier may simply no longer exist;
- former allies may refuse assistance because of what the player did.

The father, Người Đồng Hành and protagonist each have to commit to what they believe.

---

## CHAPTER XII — QUY NGUYÊN

The Cửu Mạch converge at **Thiên Tâm**.

There is no remaining purely good faction or purely evil faction.

The protagonist finally has enough power to influence the future of civilization.

But the final question is not whether they are strong enough.

It is:

> **Ngươi tin điều gì đủ sâu để bắt người khác sống với hậu quả của niềm tin đó?**

Nguyên Linh reflects the protagonist's accumulated worldview.

The final boss changes according to the player's choices and represents the extreme form of the philosophy the protagonist has built.

The player therefore fights not merely an external enemy but the destination of their own convictions when pushed beyond restraint.

---

# 7. Choice model

There is no visible morality meter.

The game silently tracks recurring decision patterns:

- one life versus many;
- freedom versus order;
- forgiveness versus punishment;
- attachment versus letting go;
- action versus refusal to intervene;
- accepting limitation versus seeking more power;
- trusting others versus controlling them.

Endings are not selected by one final dialogue choice.

The final state should feel like the logical result of the person the protagonist became.

---

# 8. Endings

## I. Nhân Hoàng — Pride

The protagonist concludes that every faction is compromised and takes control of the Cửu Mạch personally.

Linh Suy is halted. War ends. The world becomes peaceful.

But no one else retains meaningful authority over its future.

It is genuine peace under genuine dictatorship.

## II. Kẻ Giữ Mạch — Greed

The protagonist refuses to allow anyone else access to Nguyên Linh.

What begins as guardianship becomes possession. Protection is gradually rationalized into ownership.

## III. Ngày Không Còn Thần — Wrath

The protagonist destroys the system itself: Cửu Mạch, seal and access to spiritual power.

Many die in the transition, but future generations are freed from the resource and hierarchy that generated centuries of conflict.

The story does not declare whether this was destruction or liberation.

## IV. Kẻ Thay Thế — Envy

The protagonist's comparison with Người Đồng Hành becomes corrosive: why are they trusted, loved or forgiven when the protagonist has also sacrificed and suffered?

The protagonist defeats them and eventually continues the very path they once hated.

## V. Vĩnh Sinh — Desire

Nguyên Linh can genuinely resurrect someone the protagonist cannot accept losing.

The resurrection succeeds.

Its cost is drawn from the world's spiritual future.

The final image reveals distant ecosystems beginning to die.

## VI. Vạn Mạch Chi Thân — Gluttony

Throughout the game, the protagonist repeatedly justifies obtaining more power because the next threat demands it.

Eventually they absorb Nguyên Linh itself.

They become unmatched, but lose the ability to stop consuming power. The strongest being in the world begins consuming the world itself.

## VII. Thế Giới Cũ — Sloth

The protagonist refuses to impose a decision on everyone.

No Quy Nguyên. No destruction. No claim over Nguyên Linh.

They return home.

Years later, Linh Suy continues.

A younger generation asks whether the protagonist could have changed the outcome. They do not answer.

Non-action was still a choice.

## VIII. Nhân Gian

This is not a perfect or purely good ending.

The protagonist recognizes they cannot act without ego, attachment, fear, love or bias.

They reject single-person ownership of the Cửu Mạch and restructure the system so that one will cannot control Nguyên Linh.

It appears to transcend the seven previous failure modes.

Then the final challenge is stated:

> **Ai cho ngươi quyền quyết định rằng không ai được quyền quyết định?**

The protagonist still made a decision on behalf of humanity.

Sin did not disappear. It changed form.

Working concept:

**Tội Thứ Tám — Phán Xét**

This provides thematic continuation space for a sequel or prequel without forcing a specific Part II premise yet.

# Narrative rules

1. No major antagonist believes they are simply evil.
2. Every major boss needs a worldview or something they sincerely protect.
3. Cruel acts should emerge from understandable logic, not villain decoration.
4. Choices should create consequences rather than visible morality rewards.
5. Some compassionate choices must cause harm later.
6. Some ruthless choices must genuinely save lives.
7. Power is never proof of moral correctness.
8. The protagonist must develop as a written character, not merely function as a player avatar.
9. Boss lore, player progression and main narrative must intersect.
10. The game should repeatedly ask: **If you had their knowledge, losses and responsibilities, are you certain you would choose differently?**
