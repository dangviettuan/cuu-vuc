# Protagonist Bible v0.1

Status: **v0.1 complete.**

## Canon locked

- **Gender:** Male
- **Age:** 25 at the beginning of the game
- **Name:** Player-defined at New Game via text input. There is no canonical protagonist name.
- **Visual customization:** None. The protagonist has a fixed authored appearance. The player does not customize face, hair, body type, skin tone, or other cosmetic identity traits.
- **Onboarding rule:** Character setup should be minimal. The player enters a name and proceeds directly into the game experience without a character-creator flow.
- **Role:** Authored protagonist, not a blank avatar.
- **Origin:** Làng An Mộc, Thanh Mộc Vực.
- **Family anchor:** Son of a Người Dẫn Mạch.
- **Narrative function:** A human being who gradually gains enough power to affect the fate of Cửu Vực, then must confront whether power grants the moral right to choose for others.
- **Initial worldview:** There is a right choice and a wrong choice; good people protect others and bad people harm the innocent.
- **Core arc:** certainty → doubt → conviction → collapse → self-awareness.
- **Hidden origin:** As a child, he survived Linh Suy because his father used a forbidden method to implant a fragment of Linh Mạch into him. His survival came at a cost to other people.
- **Người Đồng Hành:** Diệp, a survivor ward two years older than him, raised by the Father; see [`nguoi-dong-hanh-v0.1.md`](nguoi-dong-hanh-v0.1.md). In the Prologue she is away from An Mộc, in Mộc Thành, when the well runs dry. They are reunited in Chapter I.
- **Mother:** Died in, or as a direct consequence of, the incident caused by the forbidden procedure that saved him. The father therefore carries a double guilt: strangers *and* his wife paid for the child's survival. The exact mechanism is defined in the Father bible. The protagonist does not know this until Chapter IX (U Minh).
- **Gameplay identity:** Class is chosen by the player and represents the path of combat he learns, not a change of protagonist identity.

## Canon locked — detail

> Approved from proposals P1–P12. Source tags are kept for traceability. **L10–L12 are locked as design guidance**: chapter specs and dialogue must follow them, but exact beats may be refined in each chapter spec.

### L1. Physical build and visual silhouette

- Lean, wiry build of average height. His strength comes from years of forest work and running Thanh Mộc trails, not from training as a soldier. **[Ch1: "Rừng này con chạy từ hồi chưa biết mặc quần."]**
- He must read as an *ordinary human scale* next to bosses. The art should show his size against them, never inflate him.
- **Three silhouette anchors that never change across class equipment** **[new]**:
  1. a short tail of tied-back hair;
  2. a diagonal satchel strap: the father's spare Người Dẫn Mạch tool bag, taken from the father's room in the Prologue **[Ch1: "túi dụng cụ phụ"]**;
  3. a loop of **red thread** around his left wrist, taken from the father's kit **[Ch1: "một cuộn chỉ đỏ"]**. It is the protagonist's single readable colour accent at pixel scale. **Origin:** after the childhood illness, the Father tied a loop of red thread on his left wrist every time they went into the forest, "để rừng biết con là người nhà". He reties it himself in the Prologue. **[Prose Ch.2]**
- Class gear (armour, weapon, cloak shape) sits *on top of* these anchors. It never replaces them. **[GB: class is a path, not a new identity]**

### L2. Hair / face / default clothing

- Dark hair, tied back loosely, with stray strands at the front.
- His face looks open and expressive at rest, which leaves animation room for his dry humour. He has slightly tired eyes from the start.
- Default Prologue clothing: practical An Mộc village clothes in earth green and bark brown, cloth leg wraps and simple sandals. On his hip is the father's short **brush-knife**, a tool rather than a weapon **[Ch1: "dùng để phát cây nhiều hơn để đánh nhau"]**.
- The **chest scar** from childhood is normally hidden under his clothes. It is shown only in specific cutscenes. **[Ch1]**
- Avoid: glowing marks, heroic armour, white or gold "chosen one" colour coding.

### L3. Personality in ordinary life (before the inciting incident)

- **Practical and restless.** He needs his hands busy. Waiting is unbearable to him. **[Ch1: "Chờ đợi là thứ công việc ngu ngốc nhất mà con người từng phát minh."]**
- **Dry, self-deprecating humour**, used to defuse tension. **[Ch1: "Cha mà về lúc này thì con chết chắc."]**
- **Direct.** He asks the question everyone else avoids, and he wants people to be told the truth. **[Ch1: "Nếu là thật thì họ nên biết."]**
- **Observant of people.** He reads tells and small habits. **[Ch1: he knows the father's thumb-on-finger tell]**
- **Helpful and well known in the village.** He is not a loner. People call him when something needs doing. **[new]**
- **Curious about what is forbidden.** **[Ch1: the book about Mạch Ấn]**
- **Obedient on the surface, with a limit.** He follows a rule until the rule stops making sense to him. **[Ch1: he locks the door and takes the knife off, then searches the room anyway]**

### L4. Core flaw (beyond initial moral certainty)

**He cannot tolerate uncertainty.**

He turns every worry into something he can do, even when acting is premature or wrong. **[Ch1: "Một thứ đủ rõ ràng để biến nỗi lo thành việc phải làm."]**

His early moral certainty is not naïve goodness. It is how he copes with uncertainty: clear rules mean he always knows what to do next. When the world stops offering clear rules (Chapter III onward), this flaw pushes him toward the shortcuts that each ending represents: taking control, destroying, accumulating power, or refusing to act at all.

### L5. Primary fear

**Being helpless while someone he loves is in danger.**

- This fear is why the scripted loss to Thủ Mạch Nhân **[NB Ch.V]** wounds him personally and is not just a plot beat.
- It is the root of the Gluttony path: "the next threat demands more power".
- **[Ch1]** seeds it: the silence after the roar scares him more than the roar itself, because silence leaves him nothing to act on.

### L6. Core emotional wound

**A father who loves him by withholding the truth from him.**

- He has always been the thing the father protects but never the person the father *trusts*. **[Ch1: the hand that almost touches his shoulder and pulls back; "Có thứ hiểu sớm lại không tốt."]**
- He knows he was gravely ill as a small child and recovered against expectation. The father's account of that recovery has always been brief and closed to questions. His memory of that time is fragmentary. **[new; consistent with NB §3 and Ch.IX reveal]**
- The wound gives the Chapter V and IX reveals a personal edge. Each reveal confirms that the father kept the most important truths of his life from him.

### L7. Relationship with the father — before and after the reveals

| Phase | Relationship state |
|---|---|
| Prologue | Affectionate verbal sparring. He respects the father's craft and resents the secrecy. Two-person household. **[Ch1]** |
| Ch. I–IV | Searching for the father. He assumes abduction or coercion **[NB §5]** and idealizes him as a victim. |
| Ch. V | The father left voluntarily and belongs to Quy Nguyên Hội **[NB]**. This lands as **betrayal**, and the protagonist's moral certainty turns into judgment of the father. |
| Ch. IX | The origin reveal **[NB]**. The father's compromise *was him*, and his mother was part of its price. The judgment collapses, because he cannot condemn the father without condemning his own existence. |
| Ch. X–XII | Branch-dependent. Possible outcomes include reconciliation without agreement, permanent estrangement, or the protagonist becoming the father he condemned. The exact fates are defined in the Father bible. |

### L8. Relationship with Người Đồng Hành (protagonist side only)

> Her identity, background and personality are defined in the Người Đồng Hành bible (Diệp).

- They grew up together in An Mộc. The companion is the one person whose judgment he takes seriously, and the one who says the hard thing he does not want to hear. **[NB §4]**
- **Seed for the Envy path:** from childhood, people around them trust, love and forgive the companion more easily. The protagonist notices this and laughs it off. **[new; required by NB ending IV]**
- Their split mirrors the father relationship: both people he loves end up on Quy Nguyên's side of the argument. **[NB]**

### L9. Class-selection scene and mentors

- **Prologue:** the tutorial uses the brush-knife only. There is no class, and basic combat covers movement, a light strike and a dodge. **[NB Prologue: tutorial embedded in village life]**
- **Early Chapter I** **[new]**: he cannot survive the changed forest alone. At **Mộc Thành** (or an outpost on the route into deep Thanh Mộc) he meets practitioners of the four paths: Kiếm Khách, Ảnh Vệ, Ngự Linh and Cơ Sư **[GB]**. He chooses whom to learn from.
- The choice happens **before Mộc Giác**, so that the first Mạch Ấn modifies an identity the player has already chosen.
- **Framing:** each mentor briefly states a view on *what strength is for*. These are light foreshadowing of the decision axes, not moral tests.
- **Mentor identities: TBD**. They belong in the Character Bible or the Chapter I spec.

### L10. Character state by chapter

| Chapter | Arc state | Entering | Leaving |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue — An Mộc | Certainty | Comfortable, restless, mildly irritated by the father's secrecy | Afraid, holding a secret that "knows who he is" |
| I — Thanh Mộc | Certainty | Single-minded: find the father | Angry. Someone *cut* the Mạch, so there is a villain to stop |
| II — Trung Vực | Certainty | Trusts that institutions will act on evidence | Disillusioned with institutions, but his moral map is intact |
| III — Xích Sa | Doubt | Confident he can find the option that saves everyone | Shaken. No option saved everyone, and he still had to choose |
| IV — Lôi Trạch | Doubt | Holds on to "intentions matter" | Good intentions killed people, and the companion relationship fractures |
| V — Trung Vực: Biến Loạn | Conviction (tested) | Hardened, determined to confront the father | Betrayed by the father and defeated by Thủ Mạch Nhân. He is helpless (L5) |
| VI — Hàn Sơn | Conviction | Rebuilding through purpose and evidence | Even the "saviours" of history were compromised |
| VII — Huyền Hải | Conviction | Seeks proof that someone was simply wrong | The Mạch really are dying, so doing nothing is not innocent |
| VIII — Vân Hải | Conviction | Ready to argue the companion out of their position | Conviction duel. The relationship state is locked by the player's choices |
| IX — U Minh | **Collapse** | Certain he would never have made the father's choice | His life is the product of that choice, and his worldview collapses |
| X — Thiên Khuyết | Self-awareness (beginning) | Hollow, searching | Understands that the real question is *who decides* |
| XI — Cửu Mạch | Self-awareness / branch | Faces the consequences of his choices as world state | Commits to a belief. The direction depends on the branch |
| XII — Quy Nguyên | Branch endpoint | Has the power to decide | One of the eight endings (L12) |

Note: in sin-aligned branches, "self-awareness" may be *refused or distorted*. In those branches he sees the truth and doubles down anyway.

### L11. Branch coherence — same person, different pressures

**Voice invariants (present in every branch, every line):**

1. Dry humour (it may turn bitter, but it does not disappear)
2. Directness
3. Reading people's tells
4. Protectiveness toward specific people
5. The need to act

**Dials (how the invariants express under each tracked axis [NB §7]):**

| Axis | Pole A expression | Pole B expression |
|---|---|---|
| One life vs many | Protectiveness stays personal and fierce | Protectiveness becomes cold arithmetic |
| Freedom vs order | Directness is used to challenge authority | Directness is used to issue commands |
| Forgiveness vs punishment | Reading tells becomes empathy | Reading tells becomes suspicion and interrogation |
| Attachment vs letting go | Humour is warm and nostalgic | Humour is brittle and deflecting |
| Action vs refusal | The need to act drives him | The need to act is visibly suppressed, and it shows as strain |
| Limitation vs more power | He admits he is afraid (L5) | He hides his fear behind capability |
| Trust vs control | He lets others hold the truth | He withholds truth "for their good", becoming his father |

**Writing test:** every choice line must pass this check: *"Would the man in Chương 1 say this, given everything that has happened to him since?"*

### L12. How the eight endings grow from the same starting personality

| Ending | Starting trait | Distortion |
|---|---|---|
| I. Nhân Hoàng (Pride) | Need to act + certainty | "Everyone is compromised, so someone must decide. I will." |
| II. Kẻ Giữ Mạch (Greed) | Protectiveness | Guardianship turns into possession, repeating the father's habit of hiding the dangerous thing "for safety" (the Mạch Ấn under the bed) |
| III. Ngày Không Còn Thần (Wrath) | Moral judgment | Judgment of people becomes judgment of the system itself |
| IV. Kẻ Thay Thế (Envy) | The wound of withheld trust (L6) + comparison (L8) | "Why are they trusted and forgiven, but never me?" |
| V. Vĩnh Sinh (Desire) | Fear of losing loved ones (L5) | Refusal to let go at the world's expense |
| VI. Vạn Mạch Chi Thân (Gluttony) | Helplessness after Thủ Mạch Nhân (L5) | "The next threat demands more power", forever |
| VII. Thế Giới Cũ (Sloth) | Need to act, *inverted* after the collapse (L4) | Afraid of being wrong again, he waits. The man who called waiting the stupidest human invention ends the game waiting. |
| VIII. Nhân Gian | Reading tells, turned inward | He finally sees his own tell, acts anyway, and is still judged |

## Chapter 1 draft details — resolution

- **Protagonist and Father details** (chest scar, burn scar, tell, kit, hidden half Mạch Ấn, torn book): resolved and locked in [`father-v0.1.md`](father-v0.1.md).
- **The two Mạch Ấn:** resolved by Father bible F3. The burned half is the spent half of his implanted fragment. It is a resonator, never absorbed, and crumbles at his first real absorption after Mộc Giác.
- **An Mộc details** (shrine keeper, bà Dần, headman, the yellow leaf, the regrowing roots): deferred to the Prologue chapter spec.

## Design rule

The protagonist must remain recognizable as the same person across every branch. Player choices reveal, strengthen, distort, or challenge traits that already exist in him; endings must not feel like sudden personality swaps.

The fixed authored appearance is intentional: Cửu Vực is an offline story-driven action RPG, so visual identity supports stronger art direction, animation consistency, cutscene staging, narrative recognition, and faster access to gameplay. Player individuality should come primarily from name, class, attributes, skills, equipment, Mạch Ấn, decisions, relationships, and ending path rather than cosmetic character creation.
